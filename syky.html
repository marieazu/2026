SYKY

SYKY is a community-driven platform where digital fashion designers release limited-edition collections and collectors showcase their NFT wardrobes. With London Fashion Week three months away, the team needed an MVP that supported discovery, purchase, and community-building.

Web  •  Web3  •  Fashion  •  Art Direction

Core Problem

How do we design a coherent, premium marketplace experience that works for both creators and collectors, within a highly compressed timeline and with rapidly evolving requirements?

Constraints

• Three-month window to ship an MVP aligned with LFW.
• Web3 complexity (wallet setup, auctions, crypto payments) created barriers for new users.
• Collections needed to feel bespoke, but the engineering team needed reusable, stable templates.
• Brand visuals came from an external agency and were inconsistent across ongoing internal work.

Approach

Working with product owners, engineers, and early creators, we identified a single "golden path" to focus the MVP:

• Browse exclusive designer drops
• Purchase or bid on limited-edition items
• Curate and display a personal NFT collection

This path became the backbone for scoping, sequencing, and validating design decisions.

1. Designing for a two-sided marketplace

Making each collection feel unique without fragmenting the system

Creators wanted their drops to express their individual aesthetic, but engineering needed a unified template.

Solution: We built a single layout structure but pushed asset-driven customisation. By varying artwork scale, imagery format, and motion, each collection gained a distinct identity while the underlying codebase remained consistent.

We also leveraged SYKY's "corridor" brand motif — a digital portal — by introducing subtle, abstract motion backgrounds. This reduced the flatness typical of MVPs and added visual depth without overwhelming readability.

2. Reducing barriers to purchase

Addressing crypto onboarding friction

Many users didn't have a crypto wallet, and abandoning the site to set one up created significant drop-off.

Solution: We integrated MoonPay to allow direct fiat purchases and top-ups. This removed the biggest blocker for first-time collectors and kept the entire checkout flow contained within SYKY.

3. Building community features beyond Discord

Users needed a way to express identity and connect without relying on external platforms.

Solution: We designed profile pages where members could follow each other and customise their personal NFT gallery. This positioned SYKY as a place to collect and be seen — core to a fashion-focused community platform.

4. Shipping fast without compromising quality

With only weeks left before handoff, we tightened our collaboration with engineering:

• Daily stand-ups to resolve blockers.
• Weekly or bi-weekly grooming to validate flows before sprint delivery.
• Rapid iteration to ensure edge cases were covered before implementation.

This cadence kept the scope realistic and prevented last-minute surprises.

Design system

Re-establishing consistency across a fragmented visual language

An external agency had created initial concepts, while in-house designers were producing new screens under pressure. Visual inconsistencies were growing.

Solution: I audited all existing designs to isolate the core visual identity, removed redundant styles, created reusable components, and filled missing gaps. This enabled fast parallel work while keeping the brand cohesive and high-fidelity.

Impact

The MVP launched successfully in time for London Fashion Week. The invite-only community engaged immediately, and the debut collection by Sunw performed strongly:

• One piece sold out within two days
• The auction item received bids until the final hour

The MVP established the foundation for SYKY's long-term platform, community, and brand expression.
